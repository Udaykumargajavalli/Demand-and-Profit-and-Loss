## Overview

Demand and financial planning for Beer and Non-Alcoholic Beverages (NAB) is affected by seasonal consumption, calendar effects, temperature and weather, pricing, macroeconomic changes, product and channel mix, taxation, costs, promotions, and exceptional events.

## Planning Environment

- Monthly and seasonal consumption patterns influence Beer and NAB demand.
- Calendar features such as holidays, working days, month, and high-sales periods explain recurring changes.
- Temperature and weather conditions affect consumption patterns across markets.
- Product pricing, PTR, discounts, promotions, and commercial deductions affect revenue and contribution.
- Macroeconomic changes can alter demand, sales mix, cost assumptions, and P&L outcomes.
- Excise duties and statutory charges vary by market.
- Raw-material and distribution costs affect marginal contribution.
- Product, channel, brand, and SKU mix add hierarchy complexity.
- Exceptional events, including COVID disruption, create structural breaks.

## Business Impact

Small errors in demand forecasts flow through the financial plan. Over-forecasting can create excess inventory and inefficient distribution. Under-forecasting can lead to missed sales and supply constraints.

Inaccurate price, discount, tax, or cost forecasts can materially affect Net Revenue and Marginal Contribution. The project therefore connected demand forecasting with P&L forecasting in one repeatable analytical process.
